## Design

**Strategy.** The paddle moves early to where the ball will land, waits there, and always returns the ball without reversing its horizontal direction. When the ball flies freely to the top, this shifts its orbit by 2 columns per cycle (period 18 mod 20). Every brick therefore gets hit eventually, so there are no dead loops.

**Predicting the landing cell.** The ball's column can be written as a position `u` on a 20-step cycle, with `x(u) = 0..9, 9..0`. Each (ball column, trail column) pair corresponds to exactly one `u`. For a fixed row and step count `t`, the target `g(u+t) = min(x(u+t), x(u+t+1))` can be written exactly as `a(ball cell) + b(trail cell)`. A single linear unit can therefore compute "landing column minus paddle column", including wall bounces. I solved these tables by hand around the cycle and checked that each one closes.

**Steps until landing (`t`), by mode:**

| Mode | Condition | `t` |
|---|---|---|
| Descending | row y | `8-y` |
| Ascending | y = 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0 | 8, 7, 6, 5, 6, 7, 10, 9 |
| Same row (bounce frame) | y = 8, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0 | 9, 4, 5, 6, 10, 8 |

The ascending values assume the ball bounces at the next possible brick. I checked that this leaves enough time to recover if the ball actually goes higher.

**Hidden layer 1:**
- **Units 0-5** come in pairs, one pair per mode (descending, ascending, same row). `R = tanh(8(S-p-0.5))` means move right, and `NL = tanh(8(S-p+1.5))` being negative means move left.
- **The dead zone** is the two cells that catch the ball. The paddle stops at the near edge, so a single sticky overshoot is still a catch.
- **Row 8 while descending** uses the exact target instead, so the paddle steps onto the ball's column for a same-direction return.
- **Units 6-7** are the flags descending (`y-y' > 0`) and ascending (`y'-y > 0`).

**Hidden layer 2:** units 0-5 are AND gates of the form (mode flag AND direction). The same-row gates require both flags to be off.

**Output:** Right and left get logit +10 when their gate is active and -10 otherwise. No-op stays at 0, and up, down and fire are suppressed, so the policy is almost deterministic.

I traced this by hand through a full opening sequence and wall cases, but nothing has been run. The main remaining risk is two sticky no-ops in a row when the ball is launched from a corner and needs 9 moves in 10 actions.
