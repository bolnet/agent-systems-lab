I use a predictive interception controller. The first hidden layer computes wall-reflected landing-position errors, ball direction, paddle-bounce detection, and which brick rows remain ahead of an ascending ball. The second layer selects the applicable forecast and tests whether to move left or right.

The controller normally favors catches that preserve horizontal motion, avoiding systematic retracing. Near the bottom it accepts either legal catch cell, reducing unnecessary movement under sticky actions. Upward forecasts assume a collision with the lowest occupied row ahead and update when the ball passes through a hole. Large logit margins make action sampling nearly deterministic. This is a hand-designed policy, not an empirically verified optimum.
